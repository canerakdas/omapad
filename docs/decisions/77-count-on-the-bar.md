# 77. A count on the bar that nothing on the page could take · ✅ Done · S

Asked for from the sofa, with the drawing open: *system altinda update varsa
gostersin, tasarimda bir ornegi var.* The picture is `Console OS v2 UI
mockups`' system panel, where the update is a cell of its own: a heading, what
is waiting under it, and one button along the foot.

The bar had already been counting. `System` has said `151 waiting` under its
name since a chip learned to say what a place is doing, and `Up to date` when
there is nothing - and that is the whole of what it could do. A count that
brings somebody to a page and then leaves them walking it for the row that
takes it is a notice rather than an answer, and the row was not there:
`omarchy-update` was reachable only through `System > Omarchy menu`, which is
the door out into the menu that wants a keyboard.

So `System > Update` is a tile, and the whole of it is eight lines of config.
Item 71 is what made it possible - a tile takes a `meta` now - and the rest is
this page's own arithmetic:

- **The same question as the chip, on the same clock.** The same command and
  the same quarter hour, because a tile that disagreed with the card that sent
  you here would be the surface arguing with itself.
- **`empty` is the up-to-date case**, and the tile carries no written
  `detail` at all. `checkupdates` prints a line per package and nothing when
  there is nothing, so the `awk` counts and the empty word says the rest - and
  since a `meta` falls back to that word before the first answer lands as
  well, a `detail` beside it would be a line that could never be drawn. `Up to
  date` while the command is still counting is what the chip has always said
  in the same seconds.
- **Three by three, the band's fourth cell.** A row break raises a floor, so a
  shorter tile would have left a strip under it that `Omarchy menu` could not
  reach and nothing else would ever fill. The page is four cards across and
  the door on its own row, which is what it already was.
- **No `confirm` and no countdown.** `omarchy-update` says what it is about to
  do and asks before it starts, so a guard here would be the same question
  twice, the second one asked by a page that is about to close. It opens
  through Omarchy's own presentation wrapper - the window its bar and its menu
  open an update in.

**What the drawing has that this does not is the mark.** The cell in the
mockup is bordered in the warning colour with a dot beside its heading while
something is waiting; the tile says it in words. A state on this surface is a
fifth of the accent under a tile (`Menu.qml`'s own vocabulary, and what a lit
toggle uses), and nothing here can light a tile from what a *command* said -
`on` is a setting's answer, not a `meta`'s. That is a surface change rather
than a line of config: a payload field, a delegate that reads it, and a rule
saying which metas are worth shouting about. Worth having if an update that
has been waiting a fortnight goes unread; the words are what ships.
